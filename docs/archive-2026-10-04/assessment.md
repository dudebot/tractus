# Final Tractus assessment — 2026-10-04 UTC

Decision: stop development, close all open issues without a replacement issue, and keep the repository archived. This supersedes the earlier conditional-resumption plan. Retention preserves useful code and evidence at little maintenance cost; deletion would discard that record without improving the research decision.

## The premise

Learning a compact sequence of articulatory controls is a defensible representation choice. A physical renderer can impose constraints that an unconstrained acoustic decoder would otherwise have to learn. Fewer output channels, however, do not imply proportionally fewer network weights: most weights can sit in the temporal, linguistic, speaker, and prosody representations. Reducing an output projection does not bound those components. Nor does a small control vector bound the cost of simulating each audio sample or differentiating a long utterance. These are architectural deductions, not measured Tractus savings.

Literal muscle activation adds a biomechanics problem between controls and geometry. VocalTractLab's geometric articulator/glottis controls and SPARC's estimated articulator positions are not validated muscle activations. A controller can be physically useful without recovering a person's unique anatomy or actual motion. Acoustic inversion can admit multiple solutions, so geometrical match and intelligibility must be assessed separately.

## What the prior art establishes

**TensorTract3 is the closest positive evidence.** [The ESSV 2026 paper](https://www.essv.de/pdf/2026_112_119.pdf), §§2.1–2.2 and Table 1, reports physical VTL resynthesis around 3–4% CER using inferred controls. It predicts 18 motor dimensions plus pressure and extracted pitch, expanding these into VTL's 30 controls. Its neural decoder is a separate synthesis path. The input is speech with WavLM features; this is not arbitrary-text TTS or a demonstration of literal muscle learning. ASR error rates do not establish human-rated naturalness or voice cloning. The authors also report that longer acoustic-only training can exploit imperfections in their learned decoder (§4.1). [Their project page](https://tensortract3.github.io/) distinguishes single-speaker VTL synthesis from multispeaker neural synthesis. This supports the representation premise while leaving the proposed complete product unproven.

**TensorTract2/VocalTractLab is the practical implementation lead.** [TT2's official README](https://github.com/Altavo/tensortract2) exposes speech-to-control inference and advertises pretrained weights. The standalone package does not contain the physical renderer; it directs readers to [TensorTractLab](https://github.com/tensortract-dev/tensortractlab) and [VocalTractLab-Python](https://github.com/paul-krug/VocalTractLab-Python). Its neural voice-conversion path should not be mistaken for physical voice cloning. The [backend](https://github.com/TUD-STKS/VocalTractLabBackend-dev) supplies physical synthesis code. This is a stronger place to investigate reproduction than continuing Tractus's unfinished frontend.

**SPARC addresses a different tradeoff.** [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12998) and [official implementation](https://github.com/Berkeley-Speech-Group/Speech-Articulatory-Coding) use a compact articulatory representation and neural synthesis. The repository documents 12 estimated EMA coordinates, pitch/loudness, speaker encoding and pretrained models. It is relevant to controllability and representation learning, but does not satisfy a requirement that tube physics render the final waveform.

**Other topics remain research leads.** [Chen, Lee and Sitzmann](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36737v1) address differentiable frequency-domain physical reconstruction, relevant to sequential solver cost. Their [demonstrations](https://people.csail.mit.edu/echen/vocal_recon/) do not by themselves establish a reproducible release or general TTS. [Articulatory Source-Filter TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00735v1), retained from the reset review, is an articulatory-conditioning/neural-vocoder lead, not evidence that physical waveform rendering is solved. Neither should be advertised as a ready install.

Useful search topics for future readers are articulatory synthesis and acoustic-to-articulatory inversion; decoder-guided motor learning and surrogate-model mismatch; differentiable aeroacoustics and turbulence/frication; biomechanical muscle-to-geometry models; and text-to-articulatory planning with speaker/prosody control. This is a bibliography, not a reopened backlog.

## Why stop this implementation?

The [reset evidence](../handoffs/2026-10-04T003722Z/assessment.md) establishes incomplete end-to-end synthesis and an inconclusive Task 009 smoke experiment. Decreasing acoustic loss never established intelligible speech. The smoke verdict's aggregation and convergence problems undermine both optimistic and pessimistic interpretations. More GPU capacity could extend that experiment, but it would not by itself validate the objective, repair the frontend, or demonstrate reference-conditioned speech quality.

No head-to-head runtime, neural parameter-count, human listening, or training comparison was performed. Prior-art results are their authors' measurements, not independently reproduced Tractus results. The recommendation is an engineering investment decision, not a claim that physics-based speech is impossible. Public code and downloadable-weight instructions are distinguished from successful local execution in the [ledger](ledger.md).
