# Tractus reset assessment — 2026-10-03 local / 2026-10-04 UTC

Recommendation: **stop the current TTS development and Task 009 training route**. Preserve the simulator and prior evidence as research assets. No T1 go decision is earned; T3 and T4 are stopped by their dependency gates, not by an assertion that physics-based speech is impossible. Owning tracker: [Tractus #1](https://github.com/dudebot/tractus/issues/1).

The assessment used the then-current default-branch snapshot; the initial checkout was clean. Assessment used code, committed reports, source papers and cheap nontraining checks; no new audio quality, training or platform benchmark is claimed.

The observable failure is wrong or unintelligible speech, despite an acoustic loss decreasing. `docs/week1/findings.md` records an ASR result of only “I” for the reference phrase and a wrong recovered tube for an in-model vowel; its later annotations correctly identify nonconvergence and disabled frication as confounders. A geometry mismatch alone does not establish unusable sound or prove that additional articulatory supervision is necessary. Conversely, a reduced mel loss does not establish intelligible speech. End-to-end text-to-audio remains incomplete in README and CLAUDE.md.

The only committed Task 009 report is a smoke run: 20 iterations, one seed per target, 0.3-second targets, both unconverged. It labels itself FAILED, but that is not a converged negative experiment and cannot decide the inverse problem. The target WAVs/ground-truth arrays and per-seed recovery files are absent from this checkout, so the historical measurements cannot be independently recomputed here. `python3 scripts/assess_task009_evidence.py` extracts the actual verdict code and demonstrates that one excellent synthetic row for one target earns PROVEN despite nonconvergence, and median aggregation can hide a failed seed. Its control rows are diagnostic inputs, never fresh model results; evidence is in `evidence/task009-assessment.json`.

Two recent research directions change the options but do not supply a ready replacement:

- [Chen, Lee and Sitzmann, 2609.36737v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36737v1) use a frequency-domain differentiable tube model with neural geometry and turbulence. This addresses the sequential simulation cost and optimizer representation more directly than buying a larger GPU for the existing time-domain MNA loop. Their demonstrations concern reconstruction/autoencoding; a general text-to-speech system is additional work. Paper speedups are author measurements, not Tractus measurements. [The authors’ project page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/echen/vocal_recon/) was checked; a runnable released implementation with a verified license and exact fixtures was not established.
- [Bandekar et al., 2610.00735v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00735v1) use estimated articulatory trajectories to condition a learned source/filter architecture and a pretrained vocoder. This offers interpretable control, but it does not keep a tube simulator at waveform inference. [Their demo page](https://coding-phoenix-12.github.io/ArticulatorySFTTS/) supplies examples; code/weights and their licensing were not verified. Rebuilding their training setup is outside the small assessment budget.

The smallest future discriminating experiment, **conditional on a licensed runnable implementation and pinned fixture bundle**, is a forward-only static-vowel and fricative comparison of the frequency-domain candidate against known tract geometry, then a short dynamic transition. Before inversion, require finite samples/gradients, correct sample-rate handling, stable source excitation, sensible formants and audible distinction of fricative from vowel. Use three fixed seeds, exact target hashes and per-case metrics; any missing artifact, unconverged case or nonfinite metric makes the result undetermined. Do not substitute Task 009’s median verdict. Cap implementation/reproduction at one working day and the first GPU probe at 30 minutes; no corpus training. If forward quality fails, stop. Only successful forward reproduction can reopen a T1 go decision, after which a separately written T3 platform test would measure ARM64 dependencies, fp32/fp64 parity, peak memory and iteration time before specifying T4.
